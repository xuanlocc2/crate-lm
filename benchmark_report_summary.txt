Interpretability metrics
------------------------

The CRATE-LM paper evaluates neuron-level interpretability using three
activation-record protocols:

  OpenAI random-only:
    Scores whether a neuron avoids activating on random, usually irrelevant
    text excerpts.

  OpenAI top-and-random:
    Scores whether a neuron's behavior is consistent across both top-activated
    excerpts and random excerpts.

  Anthropic:
    Scores behavior across activation quantile samples, following the
    short-context / activation-spectrum style used in Anthropic feature
    interpretability work.

Implementation in this mini benchmark:

  Script: interpretability_metrics.py

  The script extracts CRATE ISTA sparse-code activations from each selected
  layer, builds top-activation, random, and quantile activation records, then
  computes local proxy scores for:

    random
    top_and_random
    anthropic

  Important limitation:

    These are lightweight local proxy scores. They do not use the full LLM
    explainer and LLM simulator pipeline from the paper, so the values are not
    directly comparable to the official CRATE-LM interpretability scores.

  Output directory:

    interpretability_results/

  Output format:

    JSON files containing per-layer, per-feature scores and an aggregate
    summary for random, top_and_random, and anthropic.

Interpretation
--------------

CRATE-small was successfully trained from scratch on the source/tested dataset
and on two novel datasets. The model was also fine-tuned from a Shakespeare
checkpoint and evaluated on the two novel datasets.

In this setup, fine-tuning from the Shakespeare checkpoint underperformed
training from scratch on both Wikitext-2 and TinyStories. This suggests that the
Shakespeare checkpoint did not transfer well to these novel datasets under the
current fine-tuning configuration. A likely reason is domain mismatch:
Shakespeare is small and stylistically different from Wikipedia-style text
and TinyStories.


Connection to the CRATE-LM paper
--------------------------------

The CRATE-LM paper proposes a white-box language model architecture designed to
improve neuron-level interpretability. The main paper focuses on interpretability
analysis and neuron-level explanation quality.

This mini benchmark does not reproduce the full LLM-based neuron-level
interpretability evaluation from the paper. Instead, it verifies the training
and evaluation pipeline for CRATE-small, compares two training settings, and
adds a lightweight local computation of the three interpretability protocol
families used in the paper:

  1. Training from scratch on novel datasets.
  2. Fine-tuning from a Shakespeare-trained checkpoint on novel datasets.
  3. Local proxy metrics for random, top-and-random, and Anthropic-style
     activation records.

The experiment shows that CRATE-small can be trained and evaluated successfully,
but in this small-scale setting, scratch training performs better than
fine-tuning from Shakespeare on both novel datasets.
